     1→# Studio Errant Design Brief
     2→
     3→> **Version:** 1.0
     4→>
     5→> **Status:** Living Document
     6→>
     7→> This document defines the philosophy, visual language, interaction principles, and engineering direction for Studio Errant. It is the single source of truth for every future iteration of the website, regardless of whether it is built by a human designer, a developer, or an AI coding assistant.
     8→>
     9→> Every design decision should be evaluated against the principles described here before implementation.
    10→
    11→---
    12→
    13→# Execution Protocol
    14→
    15→Read this document completely before beginning.
    16→
    17→Do not start implementation after reading the first sections.
    18→
    19→After reading the entire brief:
    20→
    21→- Summarize your understanding.
    22→- Identify any conflicts.
    23→- Ask the Discovery Questionnaire.
    24→- Wait.
    25→
    26→Implementation must not begin until explicit approval is received.
    27→
    28→If there are tradeoffs between visual fidelity, performance, accessibility, or maintainability, explain them before writing code.

    32→# 1. Introduction
    33→
    34→Studio Errant is not a portfolio.
    35→
    36→It is not a personal website.
    37→
    38→It is not a creative agency website.
    39→
    40→It is not a product landing page.
    41→
    42→Studio Errant is a **digital studio** built around curiosity.
    43→
    44→The website exists to communicate an approach to making things rather than simply displaying what has been made.
    45→
    46→Visitors should leave remembering how the website made them feel more than remembering any individual project.
    47→
    48→The purpose of the experience is to communicate that thoughtful work does not emerge from certainty—it emerges from exploration, experimentation, observation, and wandering.
    49→
    50→Everything on this website should reinforce that idea.
    51→
    52→Nothing exists purely because it looks impressive.
    53→
    54→Every animation, transition, interaction, and piece of typography must support the overall philosophy.
    55→
    56→The website should feel calm rather than loud.
    57→
    58→Confident rather than flashy.
    59→
    60→Timeless rather than trendy.
    61→
    62→Minimal without becoming empty.
    63→
    64→Artistic without becoming abstract.
    65→
    66→Technically sophisticated without drawing attention to the technology itself.
    67→
    68→The interface should disappear behind the experience.
    69→
    70→Visitors should never think:
    71→
    72→> "This website has cool animations."
    73→
    74→Instead they should think:
    75→
    76→> "That felt different."
    77→

    80→# 2. Brand Philosophy
    81→
    82→The word **Errant** traditionally describes someone who wanders.
    83→
    84→Studio Errant embraces wandering as a creative process.
    85→
    86→Ideas rarely arrive fully formed.
    87→
    88→Most worthwhile ideas begin as questions.
    89→
    90→Questions become experiments.
    91→
    92→Experiments become observations.
    93→
    94→Observations become understanding.
    95→
    96→Understanding eventually becomes meaningful work.
    97→
    98→Studio Errant exists within that space.
    99→
   100→Rather than presenting creativity as moments of sudden inspiration, Studio Errant presents creativity as a slow process of exploration.
   101→
   102→Every project shown on the website should feel like evidence of curiosity rather than evidence of expertise.
   103→
   104→The website should never attempt to convince visitors that its creator has all the answers.
   105→
   106→Instead it should quietly suggest that asking better questions is more valuable than pretending certainty.
   107→
   108→Curiosity is not treated as a personality trait.
   109→
   110→It is treated as a discipline.
   111→
   112→This philosophy should influence every part of the design.
   113→
   114→Darkness represents possibility.
   115→
   116→Movement represents thought.
   117→
   118→Light represents discovery.
   119→
   120→Purple represents imagination becoming tangible.
   121→
   122→White represents clarity emerging from uncertainty.
   123→
   124→Negative space represents room for new ideas.
   125→
   126→Nothing on the website should contradict these symbolic meanings.

   130→# 3. Design Manifesto
   131→
   132→Every future decision should be measured against the following principles.
   133→
   134→## Design for memory, not novelty.
   135→
   136→The goal is not to impress visitors for five seconds.
   137→
   138→The goal is to remain in their memory long after they leave.
   139→
   140→If forced to choose between spectacle and memorability, choose memorability.
   141→
   142→---
   143→
   144→## Atmosphere over decoration.
   145→
   146→Visual effects should create emotional atmosphere.
   147→
   148→They should never exist simply because modern websites often include them.
   149→
   150→Remove anything that does not support the emotional experience.
   151→
   152→---
   153→
   154→## Motion should reveal.
   155→
   156→Animation is communication.
   157→
   158→Movement should help visitors understand hierarchy, transition, attention, or emotion.
   159→
   160→Animation should never compete with content.
   161→
   162→---
   163→
   164→## Stillness is equally important.
   165→
   166→A world where everything moves feels chaotic.
   167→
   168→A world where nothing moves feels lifeless.
   169→
   170→Motion requires stillness to create contrast.
   171→
   172→Allow some elements to remain perfectly static.
   173→
   174→Silence makes music meaningful.
   175→
   176→Stillness makes movement meaningful.
   177→
   178→---
   179→
   180→## Curiosity should always be rewarded.
   181→
   182→Visitors who slow down, observe carefully, or interact thoughtfully should occasionally discover subtle details that are not immediately obvious.
   183→
   184→These discoveries should never interrupt usability.
   185→
   186→They should simply reward attention.
   187→
   188→---
   189→
   190→## Build with restraint.
   191→
   192→Whenever uncertain between two options, choose the simpler one.
   193→
   194→Sophistication should emerge from refinement rather than complexity.
   195→
   196→---
   197→
   198→## Craft over trends.
   199→
   200→Avoid temporary design fashions.
   201→
   202→The website should feel contemporary five years from now.
   203→
   204→If a design decision feels popular rather than timeless, reconsider it.
   205→
   206→---
   207→
   208→## Let the interface disappear.
   209→
   210→Visitors should focus on ideas.
   211→
   212→Not interface elements.
   213→
   214→Not transitions.
   215→
   216→Not technical implementation.
   217→
   218→Technology should become invisible.
   219→

   413→# 7. Visual Language
   414→
   415→Studio Errant communicates primarily through contrast.
   416→
   417→Not through color.
   418→
   419→Darkness is the default state.
   420→
   421→Light is earned.
   422→
   423→The design language should resemble entering a quiet room at night where only a few carefully chosen objects are illuminated.
   424→
   425→## Darkness
   426→
   427→Black is not used because it looks premium.
   428→
   429→Black represents possibility.
   430→
   431→It allows ideas to emerge naturally.
   432→
   433→Darkness is the canvas upon which everything else exists.
   434→
   435→---
   436→
   437→## Light
   438→
   439→White should be used sparingly.
   440→
   441→Every bright element becomes important simply because the environment is restrained.
   442→
   443→Light guides attention.
   444→
   445→Never overwhelms it.
   446→
   447→---
   448→
   449→## Purple
   450→
   451→Purple should never appear immediately.
   452→
   453→It should feel discovered.
   454→
   455→As visitors enter creative work, subtle violet and indigo tones should slowly emerge beneath the darkness.
   456→
   457→The transition should be gradual enough that many visitors do not consciously notice it.
   458→
   459→They should simply feel the environment becoming more creative.
   460→
   461→When leaving the Work section, the color should gently dissolve back into black.
   462→
   463→---
   464→
   465→## Space
   466→
   467→Whitespace is not empty.
   468→
   469→Negative space creates rhythm.
   470→
   471→It allows ideas to breathe.
   472→
   473→Avoid filling empty areas simply because they feel unused.
   474→
   475→The website should never feel crowded.
   476→
   477→---
   478→
   479→## Texture
   480→
   481→Avoid glossy interfaces.
   482→
   483→Avoid glassmorphism.
   484→
   485→Avoid excessive blur.
   486→
   487→Avoid neon effects.
   488→
   489→Avoid visual noise.
   490→
   491→Textures should feel organic.
   492→
   493→Soft.
   494→
   495→Natural.
   496→
   497→Almost imperceptible.
   498→
   499→---
   500→
   501→## Contrast
   502→
   503→Studio Errant should achieve contrast through:
   504→
   505→- movement versus stillness
   506→- darkness versus light
   507→- silence versus interaction
   508→- simplicity versus depth
   509→
   510→Not through excessive color or decoration.
   511→
   512→The strongest visual experiences often emerge from restraint.

   514→# 8. Motion Language
   515→
   516→Motion is the primary language of Studio Errant.
   517→
   518→Typography communicates information.
   519→
   520→Color communicates emotion.
   521→
   522→Motion communicates life.
   523→
   524→Animation should never exist because it looks modern. Every movement must have purpose.
   525→
   526→The visitor should rarely notice an animation itself. Instead, they should notice how naturally the website responds to them.
   527→
   528→Imagine entering a quiet museum where curtains move gently because of distant wind.
   529→
   530→Nothing is dramatic.
   531→
   532→Everything feels alive.
   533→
   534→That is the emotional reference.
   535→
   536→---
   537→
   538→## Motion Principles
   539→
   540→Every animation should satisfy at least one of these goals.
   541→
   542→- Guide attention
   543→- Communicate hierarchy
   544→- Reinforce atmosphere
   545→- Reward interaction
   546→- Connect two ideas
   547→- Slow the visitor down
   548→
   549→If an animation does none of these, remove it.
   550→
   551→---
   552→
   553→## Motion Characteristics
   554→
   555→Motion should feel:
   556→
   557→Slow.
   558→
   559→Organic.
   560→
   561→Fluid.
   562→
   563→Continuous.
   564→
   565→Breathing.
   566→
   567→Never mechanical.
   568→
   569→Never robotic.
   570→
   571→Never perfectly synchronized.
   572→
   573→Nature is irregular.
   574→
   575→The interface should borrow from nature rather than machines.
   576→
   577→---
   578→
   579→## Timing
   580→
   581→Avoid dramatic easing.
   582→
   583→Prefer:
   584→
   585→ease-out
   586→
   587→ease-in-out
   588→
   589→spring animations with gentle damping
   590→
   591→Longer durations are encouraged.
   592→
   593→Typical durations:
   594→
   595→Hover
   596→
   597→150–250ms
   598→
   599→Navigation
   600→
   601→500–800ms
   602→
   603→Page transition
   604→
   605→700–1200ms
   606→
   607→Background motion
   608→
   609→Continuous
   610→
   611→Never stop.
   612→
   613→Never loop obviously.
   614→
   615→---
   616→
   617→## Stillness
   618→
   619→Not every object should move.
   620→
   621→Stillness creates importance.
   622→
   623→For example:
   624→
   625→Typography remains perfectly stable.
   626→
   627→Navigation remains almost motionless.
   628→
   629→Project cards only move when interacted with.
   630→
   631→Meanwhile:
   632→
   633→Background mesh continuously drifts.
   634→
   635→Particles slowly float.
   636→
   637→Cursor breathes.
   638→
   639→This creates contrast.
   640→
   641→---
   642→
   643→## Reduced Motion
   644→
   645→Accessibility is not optional.
   646→
   647→Users who enable Reduced Motion should receive:
   648→
   649→No cursor trail.
   650→
   651→No floating particles.
   652→
   653→Mesh movement slowed dramatically.
   654→
   655→Transitions become elegant fades.
   656→
   657→Hover effects rely on opacity instead of movement.
   658→
   659→The atmosphere should remain.
   660→
   661→Only unnecessary motion disappears.
   662→

   666→
   667→The Living Mesh is the defining visual element of Studio Errant.
   668→
   669→It is not decoration.
   670→
   671→It is the website's heartbeat.
   672→
   673→If Studio Errant is remembered years later, it should be because of this experience.

   675→---
   676→
   677→## Philosophy
   678→
   679→The mesh represents thought before language.
   680→
   681→Ideas before structure.
   682→
   683→Connections before conclusions.
   684→
   685→It should feel like something alive.
   686→
   687→Neither liquid.
   688→
   689→Nor smoke.
   690→
   691→Nor cloth.
   692→
   693→It exists somewhere between all three.
   694→
   695→Visitors should never immediately understand what it is.
   696→
   697→---
   698→

   700→
   701→The mesh begins outside the viewport.

   706→
   707→The composition should constantly evolve.
   708→
   709→Avoid symmetrical layouts.
   710→
   711→Avoid centered compositions.

   713→Nature rarely centers itself.

   735→Never pause.

   785→Not dramatically.

   803→## Cursor Interaction
   804→
   805→Desktop only.
   806→
   807→Touch devices should ignore cursor interactions completely.
   808→
   809→The mesh should respond softly when the cursor approaches.
   810→
   811→Examples:
   812→
   813→slight displacement
   814→
   815→tiny ripples
   816→
   817→temporary deformation
   818→
   819→The cursor never pushes the mesh away violently.
   820→
   821→Instead it gently influences nearby movement.
   822→
   823→Like passing a hand through water.

   850→
   851→The cursor is another living element.
   852→
   853→It should feel like a small source of awareness moving through the environment.
   854→
   855→---
   856→
   857→## Desktop
   858→
   859→Replace the system cursor.
   860→
   861→Appearance:
   862→
   863→Small white orb.
   864→
   865→Soft bloom.
   866→
   867→Gentle blur.
   868→
   869→Subtle transparency.
   870→
   871→Diameter approximately 12–16px.
   872→
   873→---
   874→
   875→## Behaviour
   876→
   877→The cursor follows the mouse using interpolation.
   878→
   879→Never perfectly attached.
   880→
   881→It should feel as though it has momentum.
   882→
   883→Very slight delay.
   884→
   885→Never sluggish.
   886→
   887→---
   888→
   889→## Trail
   890→
   891→Leave an extremely subtle fading trail.
   892→
   893→Trail disappears within one second.
   894→
   895→Avoid exaggerated particle effects.
   896→
   897→The visitor should barely notice it.
   898→
   899→---
   900→
   901→## Hover States
   902→
   903→Interactive elements should influence the cursor.
   904→
   905→Possible responses:
   906→
   907→slight enlargement
   908→
   909→brightness increase
   910→
   911→slight ring formation
   912→
   913→soft pulse
   914→
   915→Avoid morphing into arrows or large circles.
   916→
   917→Maintain elegance.
   918→
   919→---
   920→
   921→## Touch Devices
   922→
   923→Disable entirely.
   924→
   925→Touch interactions should instead use:
   926→
   927→soft ripple animations
   928→
   929→button opacity
   930→
   931→gentle scaling
   932→
   933→Never attempt to simulate a cursor on touch devices.
